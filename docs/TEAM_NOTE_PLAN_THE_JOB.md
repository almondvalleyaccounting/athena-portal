Subject: Athena — planning our accounts jobs

Hi all,

From this week Athena plans each set of accounts back from the year end,
rather than us waiting for the deadline. Here's what it is and what I need
from you.

**What it does**

For every accounts job you prepare, Athena proposes a chain of dated stages:
request the records, chase, records in (month 3), prepare, internal review,
client meeting or send for approval (month 6), approval, file at Companies
House and CT600 together (month 7). Each stage has an owner and a date. If
the client is late with records, every later date moves; if a job is heading
for the statutory buffer, it's flagged.

**What I need from you**

1. Open **Work → Plan the Job**. It lists your accounts jobs. Tick them,
   press **Propose defaults**, and you land on a review table showing what
   the default schedules for each job.
2. Open any job where the default is wrong: switch the meeting on or off,
   move a date, change an owner, remove a stage. Anything you change is
   pinned and won't be moved by the system.
3. Press **Commit these N**. From that night the stages appear on your
   **Today** tab on their dates, and the nightly pass keeps them honest.

Please have your list committed by **Friday 16 October**. From the following
week, a job that is a month past its year end and still unplanned will get a
weekly reminder from me.

**Two things to know**

- Athena already knows which clients pay for a review meeting (from the
  recurring invoices). If we meet a client who isn't billed for it, switch
  the meeting on and press "Remember for client" so it's right next year.
- Where we do the VAT returns and a return covers the year end, the request
  stage is a gap request (loan statements, HP agreements, third-party
  payroll, the director's personal tax information), because we already hold
  the rest.

**What it doesn't do yet**

Nothing is sent to clients automatically. Records requests, chases and
meeting invites are still yours to send; the plan tells you when. That
automation comes next, once the plans are in.

Thanks,
Bobby
